I am a Ph.D. candidate in Data and Systems at Northeastern University, where I develop data-driven methods for monitoring civil and energy infrastructure. My research uses machine learning on vibration and SCADA data from wind turbines and offshore wind farms to detect damage, model fatigue, and quantify wake-induced power losses, with a focus on methods that stay reliable as environmental and operating conditions change.

Before Northeastern, I earned an M.S. in Smart Cities at the University of Central Florida, where I built computer vision tools for smart mobility, including a privacy-preserving passenger-sensing system that combines GPS with deep-learning detection, tracking, and re-identification on a live public bus network. My earlier training in structural engineering grounds this work in how real structures behave.

With 5+ years of research experience, I bring together machine learning, statistical modeling, and engineering knowledge to turn raw sensor data into decisions that engineers and operators can act on.
